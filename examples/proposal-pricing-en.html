PRICING & MONETIZATION TEAM · Q3 RESEARCH

Adopt Hybrid Pricing

We ask the executive team to approve Option C, hybrid pricing at $599/month plus $32/active user (D1), for new accounts from Q1 2027, seeking your final decision today. The pilot behind it lifted NRR (net revenue retention) from 104% to 122% (D2). Our financial model projects +$1.9M ARR (annual recurring revenue), +11% (D3).

WHAT WE OBSERVED · DIAGNOSIS: PER-SEAT PRICING MISPRICES HOW CUSTOMERS BUY

(a) Customers pay for seats they never use — median utilization is 61% (D4), and 72% of surveyed admins rank dormant-seat billing a top-3 frustration (D5).

(b) The market has moved: win rate is 28% against usage-based rivals versus 46% against per-seat (D6), and pricing-model mismatch is now the #1 lost-deal reason, at 41% (D7).

(c) The 23-account pilot proved the fix — NRR 122% versus 104% control (D2), win rate +9pp (D8), seat-admin tickets −61% (D9). It also exposed two containable risks: bill surprise, fixed by caps and alerts rated 4.4/5 (D10), and ±14% monthly revenue variance (D11).

PROPOSED SOLUTION · OPTION C, PHASED · THREE MOVES, SIX ACTIONS

New accounts from Q1 2027: [1] publish the hybrid price book, $599/month plus $32/active user (D1) (Pricing); [2] build the usage-billing infrastructure, $480K with a seven-month payback (D12) (Engineering). Migration through H2 2027, toward the 122% NRR benchmark (D2): [3] voluntary grandfathered waves, seats stay the default (Customer Success); [4] spending caps and threshold alerts on every usage-billed account (D10) (Product). Enterprise guardrail, protecting the 63% flat-contract requirement (D13): [5] flat annual stays the default, usage on request only, capped (Sales); [6] sales-comp re-territorying across two quarters pre-launch (Sales Ops, Finance).

Non-goals: no repricing of grandfathered accounts; the Enterprise flat-contract default stands; no packaging changes this cycle.

DECISION REQUESTED · APPROVE OPTION C, PHASED AS PROPOSED

Upon approval: [1]–[2] go live in Q1 2027; [3]–[4] run through H2 2027; [6] completes pre-launch over two quarters; governance monthly for two quarters, then quarterly.

A4 · Adversarial FAQ — the hardest questions we expect today

Why not status quo (Option A)? Seat expansion, our historical growth engine, ran at +6% YoY in Q3 2026 versus +19% two years prior; pricing-model mismatch already drives 41% of lost deals; seat administration is the single largest support driver at 18% of ticket volume. Standing still compounds all three. (§1, §3, §4)

Why not full usage-based (Option B)? It maximizes the ±14% monthly revenue variance and breaks Enterprise procurement: 63% of Enterprise deals in the trailing 12 months required flat annual pricing. (§6, §8)

What if bill surprise repeats at scale? The two churned pilot accounts (9%) churned before mitigation; caps plus threshold alerts were rated 4.4/5 by the remaining pilot accounts. Bear case if mitigation fails: −$0.7M ARR — contained by voluntary-only migration, grandfathering, and monthly governance with a defined kill-switch. (§6, §7)

Do customers actually want this? Both halves are true: 68% of all admins prefer a predictable flat cost, while 57% of accounts above 100 active users — the highest-value cohort — prefer usage-based. The hybrid answers both: the $599 platform fee gives predictability, and the $32 active-user rate sits below the $38 median acceptable per-active-user price (versus $49/seat today). (§5)

Will the market converge back to flat bundles? Two usage-based competitors have begun testing flat "all-in" annual bundles. The phased, voluntary design keeps our exit cheap if the market converges. (§8)

What does this do to revenue predictability (the CFO's objection)? Pilot monthly variance ran ±14% versus ±4% under seat-based billing. Mitigations: the platform fee stabilizes the revenue base, spending caps bound the tail, and monthly governance tracks variance against a kill-switch. (§6, §8)

A5 · Financial scenarios & additional evidence

Scenarios (12-month horizon): base case +$1.9M ARR (+11%) at 60% new-account adoption; bear case −$0.7M ARR if bill-surprise mitigation fails and churn doubles in migrated accounts; required billing infrastructure investment $480K with payback in 7 months; sales-comp transition an estimated two quarters. (§7)

Evidence demoted from page one: 23% of purchased seats dormant for 90+ days and 34% of accounts buying at least 2x their active seat count, both tied to elevated renewal risk (§3); conjoint ranks pricing model the most important buying attribute at 34%, ahead of features 27%, support 21%, integrations 18% (§5); current baseline — 1,240 paying accounts, 38,400 paid seats, ARPA $1,540/month, gross monthly churn 1.8% (§1); competitor mix — 3 pure per-seat, 2 usage-based, 2 hybrid of 7 scanned (§4).
