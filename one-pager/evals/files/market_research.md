# TaskFlow Pricing Research Report (Q3 2026)

Prepared by the Pricing & Monetization Team for the October pricing decision meeting. TaskFlow is a B2B project-management SaaS. The question on the table: keep per-seat pricing, move to usage-based, or adopt a hybrid model.

## 1. Current state

TaskFlow currently charges $49/user/month (Pro) and $79/user/month (Enterprise). As of Q3 2026: 1,240 paying accounts, 38,400 paid seats, ARPA $1,540/month, net revenue retention (NRR) 104%, gross monthly churn 1.8%.

Pricing has not changed in 3 years. Seat expansion — historically our growth engine — has stalled: net seat expansion was +6% YoY in Q3 2026 versus +19% two years prior.

## 2. Methodology

- Closed-lost interviews: 47 deals from H1 2026
- Win/loss analysis: 96 competitive deals (H1 2026)
- Customer survey: 412 account admins, stratified by segment; ±4.8pp margin at 95% confidence
- Usage telemetry: 12 months, all accounts
- Pricing pilot: 23 accounts, 6 months (Feb–Jul 2026)
- Competitor scan: 7 products, list pricing plus 14 anonymized discovery calls

## 3. Seat utilization findings

Telemetry shows the median account uses only 61% of the seats it pays for; 23% of purchased seats have been dormant for 90+ days. 34% of accounts purchase at least 2x their active seat count — the survey shows these accounts carry elevated renewal risk. Seat administration (right-sizing requests, license juggling) is the single largest support driver at 18% of ticket volume.

## 4. Competitive findings

Of the 7 competitors scanned: 3 are pure per-seat, 2 are usage-based (per active user / per project), 2 are hybrid (platform fee + usage). Our win rate against per-seat competitors is 46% (n=52). Against usage-based competitors it is 28% (n=31) — we are losing the flexibility narrative. For the first time, "pricing model mismatch" is the #1 cited reason in lost deals at 41%, up from 22% in 2025.

## 5. Survey findings

68% of admins overall prefer a predictable flat cost. However, among accounts with more than 100 active users — our highest-value cohort — 57% prefer usage-based pricing. Median acceptable per-active-user price is $38 versus the current $49/seat. 72% of respondents list "paying for dormant seats" as a top-3 frustration. Conjoint analysis ranks pricing model as the most important buying attribute (34%), ahead of features (27%), support (21%), and integrations (18%).

## 6. Pricing pilot results (hybrid: $599/month platform fee + $32/active user)

23 accounts ran the hybrid model for 6 months. Pilot cohort NRR reached 122% versus 104% for control-matched accounts. Win rate in the 14 pilot-influenced deals rose 9pp above the H1 baseline. Seat-admin support tickets fell 61%. Two accounts (9%) churned, both citing unpredictable "bill surprise" — the mitigation is a monthly spending cap plus threshold alerts, which the remaining pilot accounts rated 4.4/5. Month-over-month revenue variance was ±14%, versus ±4% under seat-based billing.

## 7. Financial modeling

Twelve-month projection with hybrid at 60% adoption of new accounts: +$1.9M ARR (+11%) versus status quo. Bear case, if bill-surprise mitigation fails and churn doubles in migrated accounts: -$0.7M ARR. Required billing infrastructure investment: $480K, payback in 7 months. Sales compensation plans require re-territorying because usage revenue recognizes differently — an estimated 2-quarter transition.

## 8. Risks

- Revenue predictability: ±14% monthly variance is the CFO's primary objection
- Sales comp redesign creates a 2-quarter morale/attention risk
- Two usage-based competitors have begun testing flat "all-in" annual bundles — the market may converge back
- Enterprise procurement resists variable contracts: 63% of Enterprise deals in the last 12 months required flat annual pricing

## 9. Options

- Option A — status quo: seat expansion continues to stall; we keep losing 41%-cited pricing-model deals
- Option B — full usage-based: strongest NRR signal, but breaks Enterprise procurement requirements (63% flat-contract preference) and maximizes revenue variance
- Option C — hybrid (pilot model) with grandfathering, spending caps, and alerts: captures the NRR upside (122% pilot) while containing the two failure modes observed in pilot

The team's recommendation is Option C, phased: new accounts from Q1 2027, voluntary migration waves through H2 2027, Enterprise segment remains flat-contract by default.
